# MATP32 - Harmonic Analysis

MATP32 Harmonic Analysis, 7.5 credits, is an elective second-cycle course for a Master of Science degree in mathematics. The course introduces the principles and techniques of modern harmonic analysis and their application to a wide range of mathematical problems. The language of instruction is English.

The syllabus was last revised on 5 December 2025 and applies from the autumn semester 2026.

## Course content

The course covers:

- The maximal function
- BMO, the space of functions of bounded mean oscillation
- The Carleson embedding theorem
- Muckenhoupt weights
- Fundamental operators of harmonic analysis, including singular integral operators, the Hilbert transform, and the square function
- Modern discretisation techniques, including wavelets, the Haar wavelet, and sparse domination
- Applications within mathematics, including partial differential equations, and outside mathematics

## Learning outcomes

After completing the course, the student should be able to:

- Analyse mathematical problems using methods from harmonic analysis
- Give examples of important applications within and outside mathematics
- Give a detailed account of the theory behind the methods introduced in the course
- Discuss research problems in the subject and relate them to a relevant project
- Integrate knowledge from different areas of mathematics to analyse and solve complex problems
- Independently identify, formulate, and solve relevant problems
- Plan and execute qualified tasks within a given time frame
- Explain the role of harmonic-analysis methods in mathematics, physics, and data science
- Give constructive criticism of other students' written and oral presentations
- Identify the need for further knowledge and take responsibility for developing it

## Teaching

Teaching consists of lectures and seminars. The seminars are devoted to problem solving and presentations of relevant results.

The course includes compulsory written assignments, oral presentations, and a project. The project is presented through a written project report and orally at a seminar.

## Assessment

The examination consists of:

- Written assignments with oral presentations during the course
- A written project report
- An oral project presentation towards the end of the course
- Written feedback on other students' project reports

## Grading

The grading scale is Fail, Pass, and Pass with distinction.

To pass the course, the student must:

- Obtain at least 50% of the points for the homework assignments, including homework presentations
- Obtain at least 50% of the points for the project presentation and project report
- Give constructive criticism of other students' project reports

To obtain Pass with distinction, the student must obtain at least 75% of the total maximum number of points for the homework assignments, project presentation, and project report.

## Entry requirements

Admission requires:

- English B / English 6
- At least 90 credits in mathematics
- Knowledge corresponding to MATB34 Linear Analysis, 7.5 credits
- Knowledge corresponding to MATM39 Integration Theory, 7.5 credits

The following courses are recommended but not required:

- MATM38 Fourier Analysis, 7.5 credits
- MATC21 Complex Analysis 1, 7.5 credits
- MATM32 Complex Analysis 2, 7.5 credits
