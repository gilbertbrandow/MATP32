### Seminar 1 reading guide

- [ ] Read Grafakos, *Classical Fourier Analysis*, 3rd ed., Section 2.1.1, pp. 86-89 - centered and uncentered maximal functions, open level sets, and weak type (1, 1) (Exercises 1-2)
- [ ] Read Grafakos, *Classical Fourier Analysis*, 3rd ed., Section 1.2.4, pp. 25-29 - approximate identities (Exercise 4a)
- [ ] Read Grafakos, *Classical Fourier Analysis*, 3rd ed., Section 1.1.2, pp. 6-8 - almost-everywhere convergent subsequences (Exercise 4b)
- [ ] Read Grafakos, *Classical Fourier Analysis*, 3rd ed., Section 2.1.3, pp. 93-96 - oscillation and the Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem (Exercise 5)
- [ ] Read Grafakos, *Classical Fourier Analysis*, 3rd ed., Section 1.1, pp. 10-11 - local integrability (Exercise 3; consult as needed)

## Week 40 (28 September-4 October)

- [ ] Lecture 9: Characterizations of the real Hardy space \(H^1\); Calderón-Zygmund operators from \(H^1\) to \(L^1\) using atoms (relevant material: Grafakos, *Modern Fourier Analysis*, Section 2.1.1 and Section 2.4.1, especially Theorem 2.4.1; statements corresponding to Theorems 2.2.9 and 2.3.12 for \(p=1\)) (Tuesday, 29 September)
- [ ] Lecture 10: Introduction to \(\operatorname{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^d)\); basic properties and the John-Nirenberg theorem; statement of \(H^1\)-BMO duality (relevant material: Grafakos, *Modern Fourier Analysis*, Section 3.1 and the statement of Theorem 3.2.2) (Friday, 2 October)
